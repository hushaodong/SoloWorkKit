Paste the straight lengths you need, set the stock you can actually buy, and get a board-by-board plan. Timber, tubing, trim or bar — one material and stock size at a time.

1. Set up your material

Use decimals or fractions such as 30 1/2. Changing units converts all valid measurements; it does not change the physical plan. Use one material, section and grade per plan.

2. Paste your cut list

Example: 30 1/2,4,Leg. Comma or tab separated, optional header length,quantity,label. Up to 500 total pieces. Do not enter customer or personal details.

Conservative kerf rule: reserves one full kerf for every finished piece, including the last. End trim includes the material and kerf discarded when squaring each end. The planner never assumes a factory end can replace a cut.

Check the plan before you cut.

Measure actual stock, defects and blade width. Nominal lumber sizes are not measured lengths. This tool plans 90-degree crosscuts only; it does not check structural suitability, grain matching, joints, angles or machine safety.

The summary separates finished material, reserved kerf, end trim and remaining offcuts. A short offcut is still material, not sawdust. Offcuts above your keep threshold are flagged for reuse but are not automatically available in another project.

Small jobs use a bounded search after a best-fit plan. A minimum is reported only when proved for this model; other plans are marked “feasible heuristic” or “best found”. These are planning estimates, not a promise of the cheapest supplier or a guarantee against measurement errors.

No account, uploads, advertising scripts or analytics are used by this tool. Inputs stay in temporary page memory; export before closing or reloading. Hosting still processes requests as described in the site privacy notice.
